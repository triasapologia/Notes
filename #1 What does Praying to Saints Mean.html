What does Praying to Saints Mean

What does praying to saints mean? Prayer doesn't mean you worship the saints or even worshiping someone doesn't mean you look into them as a God. Even in old English prayer can mean asking for something. So as Christian you shouldn't have a problem with asking your friend to pray for you, it happens all the time. The real question is can the people who died in Christ hear us or be aware of the things on earth. When we say they hear or are aware of the things that happen on the earth they don't have to be all knowing to be like that. God can make them know those things, if someone on earth asks let's say from Mary to pray for him/her God can make that known to her, so she can pray for that person.

  1. Do people who died in Christ aware what's going on earth?
  2. Even if yes that doesn't mean they need to be all knowing (they only need the all knowing God for that).

If we can show the bible teaches people are aware of things that happen on earth, there is no problem asking them to pray for us. We need to also keep in mind that people who are with Jesus, they are not dead but living. For us they are not with us (dead to us) but not to God. So if we show Protestants the verses in the bible that shows people who are with Jesus know the things on earth, they can interpret those verses differently. If everyone can interpret these verses differently, who has the right to do it, If no one has the right to interpret correctly what's the point we have the scripture. This is where people become their own Pope.

Also certain doctrine (which is taught in the bible) develops over time. Developing doesn't mean we have a completely different doctrine, but imagine we see a kitten but after one or two years it has grown up and became a Cat. We don't say he is completely different, but the same cat has grown up over time. We experience this with the Trinity. Our understanding of things can become stronger over time. Like early Christina or the apostle themselves may have experienced the Trinity but they didn't explain it the way we explain right now. But if we can demonstrate that the Apostles believed Jesus and the Holy Spirit to have divine attributes and they are doing things only God can do it's safe to come to a conclusion that they believed Jesus and Holy Spirit to be God. That's what doctrines develop over time. Protestants have no problem with that (Trinity) but when we use the same method for the things like asking from the saints to pray for us they use a double standard.
