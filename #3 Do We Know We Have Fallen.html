Do We Know We have Fallen?

People try to argue that looking at the natural world we can come to God or know he exists. This claim somehow backed by (Romans 1:20) St.Paul: "For the invisible things of him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even his eternal power and Godhead; so that they are without excuse"

It seems people can know about God looking at the creation. That's true! but can we define God or his revelation putting this world/nature first? or do we need revelation in order to interpret the world?

Some one can come to know that there is a God who created this world by looking at the creation as Paul described. But can they interpret the world correctly? By looking at this world death seems natural, so people can come to a conclusion that death is part of God's plan. Looking at this world we see disasters. People killing each other and wars all over the globe. People are lying, deceitfully, full of trickery and selfish. With looking at these can we come to a conclusion that the world has fallen? (It's seems like we can come to a conclusion that we are fallen)

Does fall need to be accepted by faith or looking at nature? Can someone come to the conclusion that we are living in a fallen world without Divine Revelation?

Also if early church fathers and saints before the schism talked about things that align with Orthodox Church, if nowadays catholic reject those things and try to interpret the bible in order to align with modern science, Who is faithful to the Apostolic teachings? Who is more faithful to the early Church Fathers and Saints? If the Catholic Church rejects those interpretations of the fathers and reinterpret them to fit in to ever changing modern discoveries why do I need to follow that tradition? Through Adam's fall death entered into the creation, what's that mean? What early fathers understood about that? Is it a physical death with the fall of the cosmos or just a spiritual death? If it's a spiritual death that means physical death is part of the cosmos. Christ only came to recover people from spiritual death. Because he came to restore what Adam did. If Adam's fall is just spiritual, why do we need to think that Christ redeemed the whole universe? If that's the path people wanna go it's lead to compromise the truth. That may be the reason Calvinist believe Jesus only died for the elect. Cosmos to Spiritual and Spiritual to Elect.

If we look at the world (this fallen world without Revelation) death seems natural. How do we know what this world is without God's Revelation? Who in the right mind can interpret the world without God's revelation? People can try but will they come to the right conclusions?
